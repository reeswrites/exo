I read difficult books twice on purpose now, and the two passes have different
jobs. The first pass is only allowed to build a map: what the argument is, where
it turns, which chapters are load-bearing and which are the author enjoying
themselves. No marginalia, no arguing back.

The second pass is where I am permitted to disagree, and it goes much faster
because I already know which disagreements are worth having. Most of what felt
like objections on a first reading turn out to be impatience — I had not yet
seen the paragraph where the author handles the thing I was about to accuse them
of ignoring.

The cost is obvious and I keep paying it anyway. Two passes through a hard book
is slower than one pass through two books, and I have not yet met anyone who
regretted the trade after a year.
