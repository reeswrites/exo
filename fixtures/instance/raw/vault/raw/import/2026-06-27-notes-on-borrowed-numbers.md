Half the figures I copied into my notes last year came without their scale, and
a number without its scale is a rumour. Eight out of ten is a rave from someone
who rates strictly and a shrug from someone whose median is eight point one.

I have started writing the distribution beside the value whenever I record
anyone's rating, including my own. It takes one extra clause and it is the
difference between a datum and an anecdote. When I cannot find the
distribution, I write that down too, which is a surprisingly effective way of
noticing how often I could not.

The habit has made me much slower to quote a single number in an argument. That
alone has probably been worth more than every number I have collected.
