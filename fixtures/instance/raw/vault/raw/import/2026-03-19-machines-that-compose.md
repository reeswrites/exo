The engine can do whatever we know how to order it to perform. That sentence is
usually quoted as a limit, and it is one, but it is also a specification. It
says that the interesting question is never what the machine could do in
principle — it is what we have managed to state precisely enough to be worth
ordering.

Supposing the relations between pitched sounds were susceptible of expression,
the engine might compose elaborate pieces of music of any degree of complexity.
I keep returning to this because it is the smallest example I know of the real
argument: the machine's reach is exactly the reach of our notation, and nothing
about music makes it harder than arithmetic once you have written the relations
down.

What stops us is almost never the arithmetic. It is that we do not know what we
mean by "a good phrase" with enough precision to hand it over. Every time I have
tried, the difficulty moved from the machine to me, which is where I suspect it
was living all along.
