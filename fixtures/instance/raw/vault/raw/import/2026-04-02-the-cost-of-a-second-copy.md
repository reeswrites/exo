I have started refusing to keep a second copy of anything I can rebuild. Not out
of tidiness — out of a distrust of copies that drift. A second copy is a promise
to keep two things equal, and I have never once kept that promise past the third
month.

The rule I have landed on is that a copy is allowed if losing it costs a rebuild
rather than a fact. Derived tables, indexes, caches, thumbnails: all fine, all
disposable, none of them the record. Anything that is the only witness to
something that happened gets exactly one home and no siblings.

This turns out to be a question about who may write rather than about what the
data is, which is a distinction I did not have language for until recently. Two
processes writing the same file is the actual problem. The duplicate is only the
place where the problem becomes visible.
