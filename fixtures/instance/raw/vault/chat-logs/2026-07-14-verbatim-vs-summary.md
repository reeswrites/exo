**ada** · 2026-07-14T20:02
Argue me out of a rule I am about to adopt: a system holding my own writing may cut it and reorder it, but may never write a new sentence and attribute it to me. No summaries of my notes, ever, in my voice.

**assistant** · 2026-07-14T20:03
The counter-argument is retrieval quality. A thousand fragments with no connective tissue is hard to navigate, and a summary is often the only thing that makes a long thread findable at all.

**ada** · 2026-07-14T20:09
Findability I will grant. But findability can be solved with a machine-written label that is clearly labelled as machine-written, sitting next to my verbatim words rather than replacing them. The thing I refuse is the summary standing in for the source.

**assistant** · 2026-07-14T20:11
So the rule is really about attribution rather than about generation. A machine may write about you as long as nothing it writes can be mistaken for something you wrote.

**ada** · 2026-07-14T20:18
That is a much better statement of it than mine, and it makes the implementation obvious. Every span carries who authored the bytes, and anything the machine produced is a different colour all the way down to the storage layer.
