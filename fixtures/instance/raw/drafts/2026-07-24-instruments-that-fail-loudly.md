# Instruments that fail loudly

The instruments I trust all share one property: they fail loudly, and in a direction I
can predict. A balance that sticks is worse than a balance that reads high, because
the sticking one lets me carry on believing a number that stopped being measured.

I now hold software to the same standard, which rules out most of it. A tool that
quietly returns fewer results when its input goes missing is a stuck balance. It will
not tell me the corpus shrank; it will answer a smaller question with the same
confidence and let me act on the difference.

The piece I want to write is about what the alternative costs. Refusing is expensive
and unpopular — every guard that refuses will one day refuse wrongly, at night, when
nobody is awake to override it.
