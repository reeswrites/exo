I read difficult books twice on purpose, and the two passes have different jobs. The
first pass is only allowed to build a map: what the argument is, where it turns,
which chapters are load-bearing and which are the author enjoying themselves. No
marginalia, no arguing back.

The second pass is where I am permitted to disagree, and it goes much faster because
I already know which disagreements are worth having. Most of what felt like an
objection on a first reading turns out to be impatience — I had not yet reached the
paragraph where the author handles the thing I was about to accuse them of ignoring.

Two passes through a hard book is slower than one pass through two books. I have not
yet met anyone who regretted the trade after a year.
