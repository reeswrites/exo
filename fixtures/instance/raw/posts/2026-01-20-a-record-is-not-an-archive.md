An archive is optimised for keeping things. A record is optimised for finding out
what happened. They look identical from the outside and they age completely
differently, because an archive is allowed to be uniform and a record is not.

The test I use is whether the thing can answer a question about itself. An archive
of every letter I ever wrote cannot tell me when I stopped writing to someone. A
record can, because it carries the shape of its own accumulation: when each row
arrived, what it arrived from, and whether anyone has looked at it since.

Most personal systems I have seen are archives wearing a record's vocabulary. They
accumulate faithfully, they retrieve on exact terms, and they are silent about
every question whose answer is a trend. That silence is not a missing feature. It
is what you get when you optimise for keeping, and it is why so many carefully
maintained personal databases turn out to be unusable the first time someone asks
them something interesting.
