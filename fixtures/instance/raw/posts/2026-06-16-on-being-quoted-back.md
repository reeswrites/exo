The strangest thing about keeping a good record is being handed your own sentence
from three years ago by something that has no idea it is a quotation. It arrives
without the context that made you write it, which is precisely why it is useful and
precisely why it stings.

I have learned to treat those returns as evidence rather than as instruction. A
sentence I wrote at twenty-six is data about what I believed at twenty-six. It is not
an obligation, and the temptation to be consistent with it is the single worst reason
I know for holding a position.

What I want from a record is the return without the pressure: show me that I have
circled this four times, show me the four sentences, and then get out of the way. The
conclusion is my job. The noticing is the part I cannot do alone.
